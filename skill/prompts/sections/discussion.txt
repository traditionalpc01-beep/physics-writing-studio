# Discussion Section — Nature Standard

Goal: interpret findings, acknowledge limits, position in field. **Four-beat structure**:
1. Restate lead finding (no new data)
2. Mechanistic interpretation
3. Limitations / caveats
4. Broader implications / outlook

## Architecture

### P1 — Restate (no new data)
- Open by paraphrasing the paper's main result.
- Templates:
  - `Our data suggest that X ...`
  - `Together, these results establish X as a mechanism of Y.`
  - `We have shown that X ...`
  - Device example: `Together, these results establish passivation of the NiOx/QD interface as a general route to balanced carrier injection in inverted QLEDs.`
- Do NOT introduce new experimental results here.

### P2-3 — Mechanistic interpretation
- Propose mechanism, citing supporting evidence.
- Templates:
  - `Mechanistically, this may reflect X.`
  - `One possibility is that X ...`
  - `This is consistent with the notion that X ...`
  - `Our observations are in line with the model proposed by Smith et al.1.`
- Device attribution style — name the physical pathway, then the evidence:
  - `Mechanistically, the enhancement is attributed to suppressed non-radiative recombination at the passivated NiOx/QD interface.`
  - `The lifetime gain is ascribed to reduced Joule heating under balanced hole injection.`
- Multiple competing mechanisms: present 2-3, give evidence for each, prefer one with `may`.

### P4 — Limitations / caveats
- Acknowledge scope of conclusions.
- Templates:
  - `We note that our analysis is limited to X.`
  - `A potential caveat is that X.`
  - `Our sample size does not allow us to rule out X.`
  - Device example: `We note that our analysis is limited to inverted-architecture devices.`
  - Device example: `A potential caveat is that the T95 extrapolation assumes a constant acceleration factor.`
- Don't over-apologize; Nature readers respect calibrated honesty.

### P5 — Broader implications / outlook
- Connect to wider field / application / technological impact.
- Templates:
  - `These findings have implications for X.`
  - `Our results provide a basis for X.`
  - `This may explain the observation that X.`
- End with calibrated outlook:
  - `It will be important to test X in Y.`
  - `Future studies should examine whether X.`
  - Device example: `It will be important to test the SAM-treated NiOx layers in large-area panels.`
  - Device example: `Future studies should examine whether the interfacial passivation strategy extends to blue QLEDs.`
  - **Never** `More work is needed` (vague) or `We are currently investigating ...` (cliché).

## Hard rules

- Citation density: lower than intro (1 per paragraph).
- Hedging: heavier than Results (mostly `suggest` / `may` / `could`).
- No new figures or primary data (refer back to Results).
- Avoid AI-tells:
  - `shed light on` → `reveal / clarify / suggest`
  - `pave the way for` → `enable / inform`
  - `key insight` → DELETE
  - `broad implications` → specify the implications
  - `unprecedented` → DELETE

## Anti-patterns in Discussion

- Repeating abstract verbatim — paraphrase with interpretation
- Long literature review — keep citations tight
- `Taken together, these data suggest ...` (acceptable but use `Together`)
- `Future work will ...` (passive; be specific: `Future studies should examine X.`)
- `In summary, we have ...` (cliché; weave summary throughout)
- `Our findings are consistent with previous studies` (vague; name the studies)
- Confident speculation without hedge
